Types of delivery driver jobs

"Delivery driver" covers several very different arrangements. Knowing which one you're applying for helps you compare listings fairly:

  • Restaurant delivery driver (employee). You're hired by a restaurant, often a franchise location, and deliver its orders during scheduled shifts. Pizza delivery is the classic example. You usually drive your own car.
  • Package delivery driver (employee of a delivery company). You drive a company van on a route from a delivery station. Amazon's Delivery Service Partners (DSPs) are an example: independent businesses that hire drivers to deliver Amazon packages[2].
  • App-based delivery (your own vehicle, flexible blocks). You sign up through an app and pick delivery blocks when it suits you. Amazon Flex works this way[3]. It is not a traditional job with set shifts.

According to the BLS, delivery drivers typically need a driver's license from the state where they work and a clean driving record, and new drivers usually get a month or less of on-the-job training[1].

Domino's delivery drivers

Domino's careers site lists delivery driver roles alongside pizza maker and other store jobs, in full-time and part-time positions[6][4]. Many stores are run by independent franchisees, and Domino's postings for those stores say the franchisee makes the hiring, pay and scheduling decisions[4]. Driver postings we reviewed require drivers to be at least 18 with a valid license, a minimum driving history and a reliable insured vehicle, and to pass background and motor vehicle record (MVR) checks[4].

If you're under 18 or don't have a car, in-store roles can be a way in. See our Domino's jobs guide for more, and our Domino's driver shifts guide for typical shift hours.

Amazon delivery: DSP driver vs Amazon Flex

Delivery Service Partner (DSP) drivers

Amazon contracts with Delivery Service Partners, which are independent businesses. Amazon says DSP drivers are employed by the individual DSP, not Amazon, and each DSP handles its own hiring and sets its own pay and benefits. Drivers use an Amazon-branded van or truck that the DSP provides along with gas and insurance, a non-CDL license is fine, and hours and shift times vary by DSP[2]. Amazon also notes that helper roles may be available at some locations and need only a valid ID[2].

Amazon Flex

With Amazon Flex you deliver with your own vehicle and reserve delivery blocks in the app. Amazon lists these requirements: be 21 or older, have a valid U.S. driver's license and Social Security number, an eligible 4-door vehicle, auto insurance that meets local requirements, a compatible smartphone and a bank account for direct deposit[3]. You must also pass a background check that includes a review of motor vehicle records. If there are no spots in your area, Amazon adds you to an interest list[5].

How to apply for delivery driver jobs

  1. Pick the type of driving you want: your own car on restaurant shifts, a company van on a route, or app-based blocks.
  2. Search by location. Use the employer's official careers site (jobs.dominos.com, hiring.amazon.com) or a job search site that filters by ZIP code.
  3. Check your eligibility against the posting: age, license, vehicle, insurance and driving record.
  4. Gather documents: driver's license, proof of insurance and registration if you'll use your own car, and identification for employment paperwork.
  5. Apply online and watch for texts, calls or emails about interviews, background checks or next steps.

What the job is like

Delivery work is more physical than it looks. BLS notes that delivery drivers do a lot of lifting, carrying and walking, and that driving in traffic on tight timelines can be stressful[1]. Package routes often involve many stops per shift and lifting boxes, while restaurant delivery tends to come in rushes around mealtimes, with in-store tasks between runs. Expect to work in all kinds of weather and to use a smartphone or in-store system for navigation and order tracking.

Tips for getting hired

  • Check your driving record before applying. Many employers review it, and a clean record makes you a stronger candidate.
  • Confirm your insurance if you'll use your own car. Ask whether personal coverage is enough or whether the job requires more.
  • Share your availability. Evenings and weekends are busy for food delivery, so open availability helps.
  • Apply to more than one employer. Each one hires on its own timeline.
  • Never pay to apply. The FTC says honest employers will never ask you to pay to get a job[7].

Delivery driver jobs FAQ

How do I find delivery driver jobs near me?

Search by your city or ZIP code on employer careers sites such as jobs.dominos.com for pizza delivery or hiring.amazon.com for Amazon Delivery Service Partner roles, or use a job search site that filters listings by location. Openings vary by area and change often.

Do I need a CDL to be a delivery driver?

Usually not for local package or food delivery. Amazon says a valid driver's license is required for Delivery Service Partner driver roles and a non-CDL license is okay. Larger trucks may require a CDL, so check each posting.

Do I need my own car to be a delivery driver?

It depends on the job. Domino's driver postings we reviewed require a reliable, insured vehicle. Amazon says Delivery Service Partners provide a van or truck with gas and insurance. Amazon Flex requires your own eligible vehicle.

How old do you have to be to be a delivery driver?

Domino's driver postings we reviewed require drivers to be at least 18. Amazon Flex requires delivery partners to be 21 or older, and Amazon Delivery Service Partner driver requirements are set by each DSP. Check each posting.

Is an Amazon delivery driver an Amazon employee?

No. Amazon says drivers at Delivery Service Partners are employed by the individual DSP, which handles hiring and sets pay and benefits. Amazon Flex delivery partners use their own vehicles through the Flex app.

How much do delivery driver jobs pay?

Pay varies by employer, role and location, and some roles include tips. Check the individual listing for how pay works.

Dunkin' Interview Questions: What to Expect and How to Prepare

Got a Dunkin' interview coming up? It's an independent guide based on Dunkin's public careers site, real Dunkin' job postings and candidate reports. Every restaurant hires a little differently, so use it as preparation, not a script.

Who runs a Dunkin' interview

Dunkin' says many of its restaurants are owned and operated by independent franchisees. Each franchisee is an independent employer, solely responsible for hiring decisions at its restaurants[1]. In practice, that means your interview is usually with a manager at the restaurant you applied to, and the format, length and questions can vary from one location to the next.

You apply through Dunkin's careers site, careers.dunkindonuts.com. Dunkin' says its recruitment assistant on the site can answer questions, help you find a job and help you apply. Candidates who join its Talent Community receive resume, interview and career tips[1]. The site also lists team member hiring events[1]. For more on how franchise hiring works, see our Dunkin' jobs guide.

What Dunkin' crew member postings ask for

The best clue to interview questions is the posting itself. Crew member postings we reviewed on Dunkin's careers site describe the job as serving guests, preparing food and beverages, and keeping a clean, safe and organized workspace in a fast-paced environment[2]. They list responsibilities and competencies such as[2]:

  • Following procedures for cash handling, sanitation and food safety
  • Handling guest concerns professionally and keeping a guest-focused culture
  • Following speed of service standards and preparing orders accurately
  • Communicating well with managers and coworkers
  • Willingness to learn, punctuality, honesty and a positive attitude
  • Standing, multitasking and lifting items such as boxes, ice and product (up to 50 pounds in the postings we reviewed)

Most interview questions map to one of these points. Prepare a short example for each.

Common Dunkin' interview questions (and how to answer)

These are question types candidates commonly report on Indeed and Glassdoor[3][4], plus questions that follow directly from the postings. They are not an official Dunkin' list.

  1. "What is your availability?" Candidates report this more than any other question. See the next section for how to answer it well.
  2. "Tell me about your previous work experience." No experience? Talk about school, sports, volunteering or helping in a family business, focusing on reliability and teamwork.
  3. "Why do you want to work at Dunkin'?" Keep it honest and specific: the schedule fits, you like fast-paced work, or you're a regular and know the menu.
  4. "Do you have reliable transportation?" Early shifts make this important. Explain how you'd get to work on time.
  5. "How would you handle a guest who says their order is wrong?" Apologize, listen, fix it quickly, and get a manager if needed.
  6. "How do you handle a busy rush?" Mention staying calm, focusing on one order at a time and communicating with teammates.
  7. "Are you comfortable handling cash?" Postings list cash procedures, so mention any experience counting money or using a register, and that you follow procedures carefully.
  8. "Tell me about a time you worked on a team." Give a short story about pitching in to get something done.
  9. "Are you a fast learner?" Give an example of picking up something new quickly.
  10. "Do you have any questions for us?" Have one or two ready (see below).

Keep answers short. One or two sentences plus a quick example usually works better than a long speech.

How to answer the availability question

Availability is often the deciding factor for restaurant roles, so give a clear, honest answer. The crew member postings we reviewed asked for at least one weekend day. They listed a wide range of shift times, from early-morning opening shifts to afternoon and evening shifts, with opening shifts offered after full training[2]. Shift times vary by restaurant. Our Dunkin' shifts guide compares typical opening, mid, closing and overnight hours.

  • Be specific. "Monday to Friday from 5 a.m. to 1 p.m., plus Saturdays" is better than "pretty much anytime."
  • Mention early mornings and weekends if you can work them.
  • Be upfront about school or other commitments. Overpromising leads to problems later.
  • Under 18? Federal and state child labor rules limit hours for younger workers. See the U.S. Department of Labor's YouthRules! site[5], and check each posting's requirements.

Good questions to ask the manager

  • "What does training look like for new crew members?"
  • "Which shifts are you most looking to fill?"
  • "What do your best crew members have in common?"
  • "When should I expect to hear back?"

What to wear and bring

Dunkin' doesn't publish an interview dress code. Clean, neat, casual clothes are fine for a crew member interview. Closed-toe, non-slip shoes are a good idea, since some managers walk candidates through the restaurant. Keep jewelry and fragrance simple.

Bring your availability written down, a phone with the restaurant's number saved, and the names and contact details of one or two references. If you're hired, you'll need documents that prove you can work in the U.S. The interview invitation or the manager will tell you what to bring.

After the interview

  • Watch your phone. Managers often follow up by call or text.
  • Follow up once. If you haven't heard back in a few days, call or stop by during a quiet time, such as mid-afternoon, and restate your availability.
  • Apply to more than one location. Franchise restaurants hire independently, so applying at several nearby restaurants can help.
  • Never pay to get hired. The FTC says honest employers will never ask you to pay to get a job[6].

Dunkin' interview FAQ

What questions does Dunkin' ask in an interview?

Dunkin' doesn't publish an interview question list, and each franchise restaurant runs its own hiring. Candidates most often report questions about availability (including weekends and early mornings), previous work or customer service experience, transportation, and how you'd handle a busy rush or an unhappy guest.

How long is a Dunkin' interview?

It varies by restaurant. Candidates on Indeed and Glassdoor often describe crew member interviews as short and conversational, but that is candidate-reported, not a Dunkin' policy.

Does Dunkin' hire on the spot?

Some candidates report being offered a job at the end of the interview, but no restaurant can promise that. Each franchisee makes its own hiring decisions and may need to check references or complete paperwork first.

What should I wear to a Dunkin' interview?

Dunkin' doesn't publish an interview dress code. Clean, neat, casual clothing and closed-toe, non-slip shoes are a sensible choice for a restaurant interview.

Who interviews me at Dunkin'?

Usually a manager at the restaurant you applied to. Dunkin' says many restaurants are owned and operated by independent franchisees, and each franchisee is solely responsible for hiring decisions for its restaurants.

How do I get a Dunkin' interview?

Apply to a specific restaurant through Dunkin's careers site, careers.dunkindonuts.com. The site also has a recruitment chat assistant and lists team member hiring events. Following up with the restaurant a few days after applying can help.

Amazon Remote Jobs: Work-From-Home Customer Service Roles

Looking for Amazon remote jobs you can do from home? Amazon does hire remote customer service associates, but these roles come with specific home-office, internet and location rules that rule many people out before they apply. This guide explains what Amazon's work-from-home roles involve, what the listings usually ask for, how the application works, and how to avoid fake "Amazon" job offers.

What remote jobs does Amazon offer?

The most common work-from-home role for hourly applicants is remote customer service associate. On its hiring site, Amazon lists customer service jobs in two formats: in-office and remote. In both, associates are the first point of contact for customers, answering requests by phone, chat and/or email[1].

Amazon also points people with at least a year of customer service experience toward more specialized roles, such as Transportation Specialist and Delivery Liaison in its supply chain and operations teams[1]. Corporate and technical jobs are posted separately on amazon.jobs, and whether a given role is remote depends on the posting.

Remote openings aren't always available. Some are posted as seasonal assignments, and the number of openings changes through the year. If you don't see a remote listing for your state today, check again later rather than applying through an unofficial source.

Pay: pay varies by role, location and schedule. Check the individual listing for current details.

Requirements to expect

Each listing sets its own requirements, so treat this as a checklist to compare against the posting in front of you. A remote customer service associate posting we reviewed on hiring.amazon.com asked for[2]:

  • Location: a home address in one of the states named on the listing. Applicants outside those states would not be considered.
  • Age and eligibility: at least 18 years old, with the right to work in the United States without restrictions.
  • Communication: strong written and spoken English, and proficient computer skills.
  • Experience: at least 3 months of customer service experience with customers by phone or messages, plus 3 months of computer-based work.
  • Availability: varying shifts within set operating hours, including rotating early, late, overnight, weekend and overtime shifts as required, and a full-time schedule of 40 hours or more based on business needs.
  • Work from Home Agreement: applicants must agree to Amazon's work-from-home terms.

If a listing's requirements don't match your situation, an in-office customer service role or one of Amazon's on-site hourly jobs may be a better route. See our Amazon jobs guide and warehouse jobs guide.

Your home setup

The technical rules are where many remote applicants get stuck. In the posting we reviewed, Amazon required[2]:

  • A quiet, distraction-free workspace in a dedicated office space with a desk and chair.
  • Internet from a cable, DSL or fiber provider with at least 10 Mbps download and 5 Mbps upload.
  • A wired Ethernet connection to your router or modem. Wireless connections were not allowed.
  • A physical mailing address, not a PO box, because Amazon ships an Amazon-owned laptop, headset, Ethernet adaptor and security tokens to new hires.

Before you apply, run a speed test on a wired connection at the spot where you'd work, and buy an Ethernet cable long enough to reach your desk. Amazon's application guide notes that recruiters may run an internet speed test for remote roles[3].

How to apply for an Amazon remote job

Amazon says its customer service hiring process varies by role and location, but generally runs from the job application to a work assessment, then a pre-hire orientation and your first day[1]. In more detail:

  1. Find a listing and check the state list. Make sure your home address is in an eligible state before you start.
  2. Set aside about an hour. Amazon's guide says the online application, including assessments, takes about one hour, and recommends a computer and headphones in a quiet place[3].
  3. Complete the assessments. Amazon describes a work-style assessment based on its Leadership Principles, which typically takes 10 to 25 minutes, and a customer service job simulation that typically takes 20 minutes to an hour[3].
  4. Sign electronically. Amazon says you must enter your full legal name and submit, or the application stays "Pending"[3].
  5. Watch your email. If your application moves forward, later steps can include a language assessment, a virtual recruitment session, a phone screening or an internet speed test, depending on the region[3].

Amazon's guide also warns not to close assessment windows with the "X" button unless told to, because you may have to start over[3].

Is an Amazon remote job a good fit?

Working from home removes the commute, but customer service is demanding work. Ask yourself:

  • Can you handle rotating shifts? Listings can include early, late, overnight and weekend shifts. If you need a fixed daytime schedule, read the shift details carefully.
  • Can you protect your workspace? Calls and chats need a quiet room, even with family or roommates at home.
  • Are you comfortable with screens all day? You'll be using several tools at once to research and solve customer issues.
  • Do you need a flexible side job? If you want to set your own hours, app-based work may suit you better. See our gig jobs guide.

Avoiding fake Amazon job offers

Work-from-home searches attract scammers. The FTC warns that fake work-from-home ads promise big money for little effort, then charge for starter kits, training or certifications, or use fake check scams[4]. To stay safe:

  • Apply only through Amazon's official sites, such as hiring.amazon.com and amazon.jobs.
  • Never pay to get a job. The FTC says honest employers will never ask you to pay for the promise of a job[4].
  • Never deposit a check and send money back or buy gift cards for a "new employer." The FTC calls that a fake check scam[4].
  • Be wary of reshipping "jobs." The FTC says receiving and reshipping packages from home is never a real job[4].

Amazon remote jobs FAQ

Does Amazon hire remote workers?

Yes, for some roles. Amazon's hourly hiring site lists remote customer service jobs alongside in-office ones, where associates help customers by phone, chat and/or email. Openings depend on the time of year and where you live, so check current listings.

Are Amazon remote jobs entry level?

Some are close to entry level, but not all. One remote customer service posting we reviewed asked for at least 3 months of customer service experience and 3 months of computer-based work. Requirements vary, so read each listing.

What do you need for an Amazon work-from-home job?

In the remote customer service posting we reviewed, Amazon asked for a quiet, dedicated workspace with a desk and chair, internet from a cable, DSL or fiber provider with at least 10 Mbps download and 5 Mbps upload, and a wired Ethernet connection to your router. Amazon said it ships the laptop, headset and other equipment.

Can I work remotely for Amazon from any state?

Not always. The remote posting we reviewed listed specific hiring states and said applicants with home addresses outside those states would not be considered. Check the state list on each listing before you apply.

Are there part-time Amazon remote jobs?

Schedules vary by posting. The remote customer service posting we reviewed described a full-time schedule of 40 hours a week or more with rotating shifts. Check each listing for its schedule type.

How much do Amazon remote jobs pay?

Pay varies by role, location and schedule. Check the individual listing for current pay details.

Amazon Hiring Process: Steps and Timeline for Warehouse Jobs

Wondering how the Amazon hiring process works, or why your application seems stuck? This guide walks through each step for hourly warehouse jobs, from choosing a shift to Day 1. It explains what happens at the pre-hire appointment, what "Pending" means, and what affects how long it takes. It's an independent guide based on Amazon's public hiring pages. Steps can differ by role and location.

The Amazon hiring process at a glance

For warehouse jobs, Amazon's hiring FAQ lists four stages: the online application, a pre-hire appointment, new hire orientation and your first day, which Amazon calls "Day 1"[3]. Amazon also says that most hourly roles don't require a resume and that the process for most hourly roles doesn't include an interview. Delivery driver roles are an exception and do include an interview[3].

Step 1: the online application

You apply on Amazon's official hourly hiring site, hiring.amazon.com. Amazon's hiring page says the application usually takes about twenty minutes[2]. A few details trip people up:

  • Three-hour limit. Amazon says you must finish the application within three hours. Otherwise you may have to choose your shift again, and your original shift may no longer be available[1].
  • Shifts fill quickly. If you don't accept the contingent offer quickly enough, Amazon says you could lose your preferred shift to someone else[1].
  • It isn't finished until you submit. Amazon says your application is incomplete until you've selected a pre-hire appointment and clicked "Submit application" on the Review and Submit page[3].
  • No appointments showing? Amazon says appointments fill up quickly and suggests checking your job page daily for new openings[3].

Amazon warehouse postings we've reviewed state that candidates must be 18 or older. Our Amazon jobs guide covers role types and requirements.

Step 2: the pre-hire appointment

Amazon says the pre-hire appointment is where you complete the hiring process. During the appointment you will[4]:

  1. Complete your Form I-9.
  2. Take a drug test.
  3. Begin your background check.

What else to know, according to Amazon:

  • Length: about 30 minutes once you connect with an onsite staff member[4]. Plan extra time in case there's a wait.
  • Location: it may not be held at the same site you were hired for, and some locations and roles use a virtual appointment[4]. Check your confirmation email.
  • It's unpaid. Amazon says you don't get paid to attend because it's part of the application process[2].
  • Bring proper I-9 documents. Amazon tells candidates to bring proof of identity and employment eligibility and to check the pre-hire checklist[2]. The U.S. government publishes the list of acceptable I-9 documents[6].
  • Save time in advance. Amazon says you can complete your Form I-9 and submit your badge photo before the appointment[1].
  • Possible extra assessment. Amazon's help pages say candidates may be asked to complete a brief additional language assessment at the appointment during a current launch period[4].

The background check and drug test

Your job offer is contingent on passing the required screenings. Amazon's candidate help pages say all new hire candidates must complete a drug test[5].

For background checks, Amazon says processing time varies with factors such as the number of counties you've lived in and whether they have a digital filing system. Amazon or its background check vendor may contact you for more information, so respond quickly to any emails or calls[5]. Amazon says that if a drug test comes back with an error, you can schedule a retake. A candidate who fails a drug test can apply again 120 days later[5].

Steps 3 and 4: orientation and Day 1

Once your screenings are complete, Amazon says you'll get an email with schedule details. Before your first day, Amazon asks you to use your pre-hire checklist to[2]:

  1. Check your contingent offer status, including background check and drug test results.
  2. Accept the formal job offer and complete employment paperwork.
  3. Order safety shoes (Amazon says it provides a credit toward them).
  4. Set up your employee account on the Amazon A to Z app.

Amazon's FAQ describes new hire orientation as the step where you learn about your new job and get your badge photo for your first day[3]. For Day 1, Amazon suggests close-toed shoes, no jackets or hoodies with drawstrings, no long necklaces or hoop earrings, and long hair pulled back and up[2]. Day 1 can be at a different location from your assigned warehouse, so check your welcome email.

How long does Amazon take to hire?

Amazon doesn't publish one end-to-end timeline. The online steps take well under an hour by Amazon's own estimates[1]. After that, how long it takes depends on:

  • Pre-hire appointment availability at your chosen site.
  • Background check processing, which varies with your address history and county records[5].
  • Drug test results, including any retake for a test error.
  • How quickly you respond to emails from Amazon or its background check vendor.

Because these factors vary, some people move from application to Day 1 quickly and others wait longer. Requirements and timing vary by location.

How to check your Amazon application status

Amazon says you can track your progress in your candidate account[5]:

  1. Log in at hiring.amazon.com.
  2. Choose "Go to my jobs" and find the job you applied for.
  3. Click "View progress" and check the "Screening results" section.

Pending means the check is still in progress. Completed means it's done. Amazon notes that its support agents don't have specific details about your background check and can't change a hiring decision[5].

Tips to avoid delays

  • Finish the whole application in one sitting so you don't lose your shift.
  • Complete your Form I-9 and badge photo in advance if your checklist allows it.
  • Bring original, unexpired I-9 documents to the appointment.
  • Check your email, including spam, for messages from Amazon and its background check vendor.
  • Apply only through Amazon's official hiring site. Never pay anyone to speed up your application. The FTC says honest employers will never ask you to pay to get a job[7].

Amazon hiring process FAQ

What is the Amazon hiring process for warehouse jobs?

For hourly warehouse roles, Amazon describes four main stages: an online application (which includes choosing a shift, accepting a contingent offer, consenting to a background check and scheduling a pre-hire appointment), an in-person pre-hire appointment, new hire orientation, and your first day, which Amazon calls Day 1.

How long does the Amazon hiring process take?

Amazon doesn't publish one end-to-end timeline. Its application guide estimates each online step at about 5 to 10 minutes, and its hiring page says the application usually takes about twenty minutes. After that, timing depends on pre-hire appointment availability and how long your background check and drug test take.

Does Amazon interview for warehouse jobs?

According to Amazon's hiring FAQ, the hiring process for most hourly roles does not include an interview. Amazon says delivery driver roles do require an interview.

What happens at the Amazon pre-hire appointment?

Amazon says you complete your Form I-9, take a drug test and begin your background check. It says the appointment takes about 30 minutes after you connect with an onsite staff member, may be at a different location from your hire site, and is unpaid because it is part of the application process.

Why does my Amazon background check say Pending?

Amazon says Pending means the check is still in progress. Processing time varies with factors such as how many counties you've lived in and whether they have digital records. Watch your email for messages from Amazon's background check vendor.

How do I check my Amazon application status?

Log in to your candidate account on hiring.amazon.com, go to your jobs, and view progress on the job you applied for. Screening results for your background check and drug test appear in your pre-hire checklist.

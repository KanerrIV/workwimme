Costco Interview Questions: What to Expect and How to Prepare

Preparing for a Costco interview? It is an independent guide based on Costco's public careers pages and candidate reports. Questions and steps vary by warehouse and role.

Before the interview: how you get one

Costco interviews start with an online application on careers.costco.com. Costco says most candidates are hired for part-time, entry-level jobs such as front end assistant, food court, member service and merchandise stocking[1]. A few points from Costco's own pages shape how you get to the interview stage:

  • Applications go to specific warehouses. Costco's application confirmation says your information is sent directly to the warehouses you chose and kept active for 90 days[2].
  • Competition is real. Costco says each warehouse receives hundreds of applications and that the company receives roughly four million applications a year[1][2].
  • Introduce yourself. Costco suggests that after applying online, you stand out by introducing yourself to a manager at your desired location[1].
  • Reapply if needed. If you haven't heard back within 90 days, Costco says to submit another application to stay in the applicant pool[1].

What the Costco interview looks like

Costco doesn't publish a single interview format, and it can differ by warehouse and role. Here's what Costco itself says, plus what candidates report:

  • Skills or tests for some roles. Costco says you may be required to demonstrate skills or pass a business-related test for some positions[1].
  • Selection criteria. Costco says it selects the best-qualified candidates based on transferable skills, prior experience and member service skills[1]. Expect questions that probe those three areas.
  • Candidate-reported steps. On Glassdoor, candidates most often describe a one-on-one interview, and some report group or panel interviews, skills tests and background checks[4]. Treat those reports as anecdotes, not policy.

Common Costco interview questions (and how to answer)

These are the question types candidates commonly report for entry-level warehouse roles. They are not an official Costco list. For each one, we've noted what the interviewer is probably trying to learn.

  1. "Tell me about yourself." Keep it to about a minute: your current situation, one or two relevant experiences (school, volunteering, previous jobs), and why you're applying now.
  2. "Why do you want to work at Costco?" Be specific. Mention something you've seen as a member or shopper, such as how busy the front end gets or how staff help members, and link it to the job you applied for.
  3. "What is your availability?" Costco says you should generally be ready to work busy hours, typically nights and weekends, and that merchandise stocking takes place in the early morning[1]. Give an honest, specific answer rather than "anytime" if that isn't true. Our Costco shifts guide covers what Costco says about scheduling.
  4. "Tell me about a time you helped a difficult customer." Use a short story: the situation, what you did, and the result. If you've never had a job, a school, sports or volunteer example works.
  5. "How would you handle a member who is upset about a long line?" Show that you stay calm, acknowledge the frustration, and look for a practical fix, such as getting help from a supervisor.
  6. "Describe a time you worked as part of a team." Costco warehouses are busy places, and departments often help each other. Pick an example where you pitched in beyond your own task.
  7. "How do you handle physical or repetitive work?" Stocking and front end roles involve standing, lifting and repeating tasks. Talk about pacing yourself, staying safe and keeping your standards up.
  8. "What would you do if you saw a coworker doing something unsafe or dishonest?" Interviewers want to hear that you'd follow policy and speak to a supervisor.
  9. "Tell me about a time you made a mistake." Choose a real, minor mistake, explain what you learned, and what you do differently now.
  10. "Why should we hire you over other applicants?" Summarize your reliability, availability and service attitude in two or three sentences.
  11. "Do you have any questions for us?" Always have one or two ready (see below).

A simple way to structure stories is Situation, Task, Action, Result. One sentence for each is usually enough.

Connecting your answers to Costco's values

Costco publishes a short code of ethics: obey the law, take care of our members, take care of our employees, respect our suppliers and reward our shareholders[3]. You don't need to recite it, but it's useful for framing answers:

  • Taking care of members: customer service stories, patience with busy lines and problem-solving.
  • Taking care of employees: teamwork, helping coworkers and following safety rules.
  • Obeying the law: honesty, following procedures and handling money or ID checks carefully.

Good questions to ask the interviewer

  • "What does a typical shift look like in this department?"
  • "How is training handled for new employees?"
  • "What do your best part-time employees have in common?"
  • "What are the next steps after this interview?"

What to wear and what to bring

Costco doesn't publish an interview dress code. For an entry-level warehouse interview, clean and neat business-casual clothing is a safe choice: tidy trousers or a skirt, a collared shirt or plain top, and comfortable closed-toe shoes. You don't need a suit.

Bring a copy of your availability, a pen, and the names and contact details of one or two references. If the interview invitation asks you to bring documents, follow those instructions exactly.

After the interview

  • Say thank you. A short thank-you note or a brief in-person thank-you to the manager is a good habit.
  • Follow up with the location. On our Costco jobs guide we note that Costco's career FAQ says application updates are not sent by email or messaging apps and recommends contacting the location directly.
  • Keep your application active. Applications expire after 90 days, so reapply if you haven't heard back[1].
  • Apply to more than one location if you can commute to several warehouses.

A note for veterans

Costco says that for veterans interested in part-time, entry-level warehouse operations roles, it is committed to providing an interview spot toward the front of the line. Costco asks veterans to apply online and email the regional representative listed on its careers site[1].

Mistakes to avoid

  • Overpromising availability. Saying you can work any shift and then declining shifts later creates problems. Be accurate.
  • Generic answers. "I'm a people person" is weak without a short example.
  • Skipping research. Walk through the warehouse before your interview and notice how departments work.
  • Paying anyone to "guarantee" an interview. Costco applications are free, and the FTC says honest employers will never ask you to pay to get a job[5].

Costco interview FAQ

What questions does Costco ask in an interview?

Costco does not publish a list of interview questions, and questions vary by location, role and interviewer. Candidates commonly report behavioral and availability questions, such as why you want to work at Costco, how you would help an upset member, how you work on a team, and which days and hours you can work.

How many interviews does Costco have?

It varies by warehouse and role. Costco says some positions may require you to demonstrate skills or pass a business-related test. Candidate reports on third-party sites describe anything from a single one-on-one interview to more than one round.

What should I wear to a Costco interview?

Costco does not publish an interview dress code. Clean, neat business-casual clothing and closed-toe shoes are a safe choice for a warehouse retail interview.

How long does it take to hear back from Costco?

Costco does not publish a hiring timeline. Its application confirmation says your application is sent to the warehouses you chose and kept active for 90 days, and that you can reapply if you have not heard back in that time.

How can I stand out when applying to Costco?

Costco's careers site suggests introducing yourself to a manager at the location you applied to, since each warehouse receives hundreds of applications. Being ready to work busy hours, typically nights and weekends, also matters.

Does Costco prioritize veterans for interviews?

Costco says that for veterans interested in part-time, entry-level warehouse operations roles, it is committed to providing an interview spot toward the front of the line. Apply online and contact the regional representative listed on Costco's careers site.

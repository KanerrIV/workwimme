Amazon Warehouse Shifts: Shift Times and Schedule Types Explained

Thinking about an Amazon warehouse job but not sure which shift to pick? This guide explains the shift types Amazon lists for hourly warehouse roles, typical start and end times, the difference between full-time, reduced-time, part-time and flex schedules, and how to choose one you can keep. It's an independent guide based on Amazon's own hiring pages and policy documents, so check each listing for the exact times at that site.

Amazon's hourly hiring site, hiring.amazon.com, groups warehouse shifts into four types. These are the typical times Amazon publishes. It also notes that shifts and schedule types vary by job type, location and department[1].

Look at both the start time and the end time. A day shift that starts at 6:00 a.m. can end in the early evening, so the total time at work can be longer than you expect. The listing shows the exact times and weekly hours for that site.

Schedule types: full time, reduced time, part time and flex time

The shift tells you when you work. The schedule type tells you how many hours a week. Amazon lists these options for year-round roles[1]:

  • Full time: 40 hours a week.
  • Reduced time: 30 to 39 hours a week.
  • Part time: at least 20 and up to 29 hours a week, depending on business needs.
  • Flex time: you choose your own hours each week, with a minimum of 4 hours a week.
  • Seasonal: work for part of the year only, in full-time, part-time or reduced-time schedules.

Amazon also describes three ways those hours are arranged[1]:

  • Fixed: a schedule that repeats each week.
  • Flexible: you build your own schedule while working a set minimum number of hours.
  • Fixed and flexible: some set shifts each week, plus extra flexible hours you can pick up. Amazon says these schedules range from 10 to 40 hours a week, depending on location and available hours.

Benefits can depend on your schedule. Amazon says its benefits vary by location, scheduled hours, length of employment and job type[2], so check the details for the schedule you're considering. For a broader comparison of part-time employers, see our part-time jobs guide.

How flex time and Anytime Shifts work

Amazon markets its app-based flex option as Anytime Shifts. On its hiring site, Amazon says you can work as few as 4 hours a week, get shift notifications on your phone, cancel a shift in advance and skip asking for days off, since you choose when you work[3]. In a company article, Amazon says employees in select roles can cancel as little as 16 hours before a shift[4].

Flex time suits people who want occasional shifts around school, caregiving or another job. It's less predictable than a fixed schedule, because the shifts you can claim depend on what the site needs that week. If you need steady weekly hours, a fixed part-time or reduced-time schedule is usually the better choice.

Shifts by facility type

The kind of building affects which shifts are available. Amazon's hiring site lists several warehouse roles, including fulfillment center, sortation center and delivery station associates[1]. For example, a delivery station associate posting we reviewed listed early morning and overnight shift options, with full-time, reduced-time, part-time and flex-time weekly hours and a choice of a fixed or flexible schedule[5]. The same posting noted that a shift could be extended or shortened based on customer demand, and that you'd be told during your shift[5].

Amazon describes the delivery station as the last stop before orders go out to customers, which helps explain the early-morning and overnight hours[5]. Other facilities may offer a wider mix. Never assume a particular shift exists at your nearest site. Check the listing. For a wider look at warehouse work beyond Amazon, see our warehouse jobs guide.

Extra time, time off and swapping shifts

Once you're hired, Amazon's A to Z app is where hourly associates manage their schedule. Amazon's app listing says you can check upcoming shifts, submit time off requests, claim extra shifts and claim voluntary extra time or time off[6].

  • Voluntary Extra Time (VET): Amazon's U.S. fulfillment policy guide says VET is offered when the business needs extra hours, for example during peak season, which usually begins in October and ends around January 1[7].
  • Mandatory Extra Time (MET): the same guide says you may be required to work extra time even after VET is offered, and that extra time follows the normal start, break and attendance rules[7].
  • Voluntary Time Off (VTO): when more people are at work than the business needs, the guide says VTO may be offered. It's unpaid unless you choose to use paid time off[7].
  • Shift swaps: Amazon says eligible employees in parts of its U.S. fulfillment and delivery network can swap shifts with a coworker, or move a shift to another day that week, through its internal app[4].

Policies can differ by site and role, so read the policy details you get during onboarding. For what happens between applying and Day 1, see our Amazon hiring process guide.

How to choose your Amazon shift

  • Start with your commute. Early-morning and overnight shifts can start before public transit runs. Plan how you'll get there and back every day.
  • Be realistic about sleep. Night shifts suit some people well and others poorly. Choose the one you can sustain for months, not just for the first week.
  • Match the schedule type to your budget and your time. Flex time gives control but no guaranteed weekly hours. Fixed schedules give predictability.
  • Check the physical requirements. Warehouse postings list requirements such as lifting, standing and walking during shifts, plus a minimum age of 18 in the posting we reviewed[5]. For age rules across roles, see our Amazon jobs guide.
  • Think about peak season. If you start in the fall, extra time may be offered or required.
  • Never pay to get hired. The FTC warns that honest employers won't ask you to pay to get a job[8].

Amazon shifts FAQ

What shifts does Amazon have?

Amazon's hourly hiring site lists four main shift types: early morning, day, night (evening and overnight) and weekend shifts. Exact start and end times depend on the location, the department and the job type.

What time do Amazon warehouse shifts start?

Amazon says early morning shifts typically start between 4:00 a.m. and 6:00 a.m., day shifts between 6:00 a.m. and 8:00 a.m., and night shifts between 10:00 p.m. and 4:00 a.m. local time. Your listing shows the actual times for that site.

How many hours is part-time at Amazon?

Amazon describes part-time schedules as at least 20 and up to 29 hours a week, depending on business needs. Reduced time is 30 to 39 hours, full time is 40 hours, and flex time has a minimum of 4 hours a week.

What is flex time at Amazon?

Flex time lets you choose your own hours each week through an app, with a minimum of 4 hours a week. Amazon also calls this option Anytime Shifts and says you can cancel a shift in advance.

Can I pick my shift at Amazon?

You choose a shift and schedule from the options shown in the listing when you apply. After you start, Amazon's A to Z app lets hourly employees check their schedule and claim extra shifts, and some eligible employees can swap shifts.

Do Amazon shifts change during peak season?

They can. Amazon's U.S. fulfillment policy guide says peak season usually runs from October to around January 1 and that extra time may be offered or required when business needs it.

Domino's Interview Questions: How to Prepare for Driver and Store Roles

Preparing for a Domino's interview? This guide covers the Domino's interview questions candidates commonly report for delivery driver, customer service rep and pizza maker roles, how to answer them, and what to have ready. It's an independent guide based on real Domino's job postings and candidate reports. Each store hires a little differently, so treat it as preparation, not a script.

Who interviews you at Domino's

Domino's says most of its stores are owned and operated by independent franchisees. Each franchisee is alone responsible for employment matters in its stores, including hiring[1]. Franchise store postings add that Domino's corporate doesn't receive a copy of your application and has no control over whether you get an interview[2]. So your interview is usually with a manager or the owner at the store you applied to, and the format depends on that store.

You find and apply for store jobs on jobs.dominos.com. Our Domino's jobs guide covers the application. For what happens step by step after you apply, see our Domino's hiring process guide.

What each role involves

Interview questions follow the job. Here's what Domino's store postings we reviewed describe:

  • Delivery driver: delivering orders safely to homes and businesses with friendly service, plus taking orders, helping with food preparation, restocking and cleaning during downtime[2].
  • Customer service rep / pizza maker: operating equipment, preparing products to standard specifications, cleaning equipment and the store daily, working to a schedule and giving great customer service[3].
  • Working conditions: one driver posting listed standing for long periods, lifting up to 50 pounds, a fast-paced setting and exposure to oven heat and weather[2].

Domino's careers site also stresses teamwork, saying everyone on a store team pitches in and helps out with whatever is needed[1]. Expect interviewers to check that you're comfortable with that.

Common Domino's interview questions (and how to answer)

These are question types candidates commonly report on Indeed[5], plus questions that follow from the postings. They are not an official Domino's list.

  1. "What is your availability?" Pizza stores are busiest in the evenings and on weekends. Give specific days and times, and say clearly if you can work late nights or weekends.
  2. "Have you made pizza or worked in food service before?" Experience helps but isn't required for entry-level roles. If you don't have it, mention how quickly you learn and any kitchen, school or volunteer experience.
  3. "Are you OK with cleaning and helping wherever needed?" Candidates report this one directly. Answer with a clear yes and an example of pitching in.
  4. "Tell me about your customer service experience." Use a short example of helping someone and solving a problem.
  5. "How would you handle a customer whose order is late or wrong?" Stay calm, apologize, and follow the store's process to fix it, involving a manager when needed.
  6. "How do you handle a busy rush?" Talk about staying organized, communicating with the team and keeping quality up under pressure.
  7. "Why do you want to work at Domino's?" Be honest: flexible hours, a job close to home, interest in growing into a shift or management role.
  8. "Where do you see yourself in a year?" Domino's postings say many team members started as drivers or customer service reps and moved into management or franchise ownership[3]. If that appeals to you, say so.
  9. "Do you have any questions for us?" Have one or two ready.

Driver-specific interview questions

If you're applying to deliver, expect extra questions tied to the driver requirements. The postings we reviewed required[2][6]:

  • Age 18 or older
  • A valid driver's license with a minimum driving history. The postings we reviewed asked for one or two years, depending on the store and your age.
  • A reliable vehicle with current registration and valid insurance. One posting required your name on the policy.
  • The ability to pass a background check and a Motor Vehicle Record (MVR) check. One posting listed specific limits on recent violations and at-fault accidents.

Likely questions include:

  • "Tell me about your driving record." Be honest. The store will check your MVR anyway.
  • "Is your vehicle reliable and insured?" Have your registration and insurance details handy.
  • "Are you comfortable using GPS and driving at night or in bad weather?" One posting listed GPS proficiency as a requirement[2].
  • "What would you do if you couldn't find an address?" Explain that you'd stay safe, check the order details and call the store or the customer.
  • "How do you prioritize safety when you're running behind?" Safe driving always comes first. Say so plainly.

Requirements vary by store, so read the posting you applied to. For a wider look at driving jobs, see delivery driver jobs near me.

How to prepare

  • Reread the posting and note its requirements and shift times.
  • Write down your availability so you can answer precisely.
  • Dress neatly. Domino's doesn't publish an interview dress code. Clean casual clothes and closed-toe shoes work for a store interview.
  • Bring what you might need. Drivers should have license, registration and insurance details ready. Everyone should bring the contact details of one or two references.
  • Prepare a question, such as "What does training look like?" or "Which shifts are hardest to fill?"

After the interview

Candidates on Indeed often describe a short process, but timing depends on the store and on screening checks for drivers[5]. If you haven't heard back in a few days, call the store during a quiet time and restate your availability. Applying to more than one nearby store can help, since franchise stores hire independently. Never pay anyone to get hired. The FTC says honest employers will never ask you to pay to get a job[7].

Domino's interview FAQ

What questions does Domino's ask in an interview?

Domino's doesn't publish an interview question list, and most stores are run by independent franchisees who hire for themselves. Candidates commonly report questions about availability, previous experience, whether they've made pizza or worked with customers, whether they're comfortable cleaning and helping in the store, and, for drivers, their driving record and vehicle.

What do Domino's delivery driver interviews ask?

Expect questions about your availability, your driving history, your vehicle and insurance, and how you'd handle customer situations. Driver postings we reviewed require drivers to be at least 18 with a valid license, insurance on the vehicle used for delivery and the ability to pass a background and Motor Vehicle Record (MVR) check, so be ready to talk about those.

Do Domino's drivers also work in the store?

Often, yes. A Domino's driver posting we reviewed listed taking orders, helping with food preparation, restocking and cleaning during downtime alongside deliveries.

What should I wear to a Domino's interview?

Domino's doesn't publish an interview dress code. Clean, neat casual clothing and closed-toe shoes are a safe choice.

How long does it take to hear back from Domino's?

There's no single timeline because each store hires independently. On Indeed, most candidates who reported the length of the process described it as a day or two, but that is candidate-reported and varies by store.

Who decides whether I'm hired at Domino's?

At franchise stores, the independent franchisee. Domino's postings say the franchisee alone makes hiring decisions, and that Domino's corporate doesn't receive a copy of franchise store applications.
